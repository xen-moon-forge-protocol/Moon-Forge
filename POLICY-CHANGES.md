# Policy changes

How the oracle's reward rules change, when a change applies, and the record of every change so far. The program's own rules are not policy: today the 10% epoch bound, the 6-day interval, vesting and fees; with the final program upgrade, planned for 2026-12-02 (moved from 2026-11-25 on 2026-11-26, [docs/UPGRADE-2026-11.md](docs/UPGRADE-2026-11.md)), also the 48-hour claim hold and the extra pot's bounds. They change only by a program upgrade while an upgrade authority exists, and never after the freeze ([IMMUTABILITY.md](IMMUTABILITY.md)).

## What is oracle policy

Everything the oracle computes off-chain before it publishes an epoch: how a burn is priced, the per-chain curve, the weekly capacity and its daily tranches, the order in which burns are paid, the extra-pot formula, the dust and claim behaviour. The rules for every burn not paid in the last v2 epoch are in [docs/spec/REWARDS-V3.md](docs/spec/REWARDS-V3.md) and in the oracle code they restate. Each epoch file says which rules built it (`version`, and for the extra pot the `policy` block); `npm run verify` recomputes it with those rules.

**The chain list.** Which chains the oracle reads is open-source oracle configuration, not a program rule: today Base (8453), Optimism (10), Polygon (137), BSC (56) and Avalanche (43114), the portals in [deployments/portals.json](deployments/portals.json). Moon Forge launched with these five chains, and this project adds none. A change to the chain list is treated as an oracle policy change under commitment 1: if a community operator who holds the oracle seat ever adds a chain, it does so with a public notice at least two epochs ahead, and every epoch file lists the chains it read ([OPERATORS.md](OPERATORS.md#add-a-chain)). A separate instance with its own program, portals and pool is a fork ([FORK.md](FORK.md)).

## The commitments

1. **Announced ahead.** A change to the reward rules is published in this file, on the site and in the project's announcements at least **two epochs** before the first epoch it applies to, with the date it applies from. Wording-only corrections of this file that change no number need no notice. This rule applies to every change after the v3 switch. The v3 switch itself is announced under the earlier published rule (at least one full epoch ahead, with the last v2 publish transaction), as the switch record below shows.
2. **Never retroactive.** A change never alters an epoch already published, a burn already made before it applies, a quote the site or the kit already showed for a burn made before it applies, or a rebate already counted.
3. **Defined by date and transaction, never by a future epoch number.** A boundary is "burns not paid in the epoch published by transaction X" or "from the epoch published on or after date D", because a week without burns publishes no epoch and numbers would shift.
4. **Versioned.** A change that alters what the files contain gets a new file version or a new field, documented in the epoch-file format page (`docs/EPOCH-FILES.md`, published with the first v3 epoch); old files keep verifying under their own rules forever.
5. **Never above the program's bounds.** No policy can push an epoch above 10% of the free reward pool or shorten the 6-day interval; from the final upgrade on, none can push an extra epoch above 8% of the extra pot or shorten the 48-hour hold.
6. **Who proposes.** While the project is run by its builder, the operator proposes changes. After the operator leaves, whoever holds the oracle seat (from the final upgrade on) publishes under the rules in force; a new holder can change the policy only with the same notice.
7. **Signals that can trigger a proposal.** The weekly metrics (`docs/METRICS.md`, published with the first v3 epoch) name the conditions that would lead to a proposal (for example one chain taking most of the capacity for several weeks). They only ever lead to a proposal under rule 1, never to an immediate change.

### Notice of 2026-11-23 (as published)

> **Moon Forge reward rules v3, from the epoch published on or after 2026-11-29.** Burns that are not paid in the epoch published by transaction `4XyLDEHiP4upPW7SEiMCDQrNrRjHGAiPjw6cKv7wN4we9uFGrByEmXtDGto5edJniVT16rQ8SprwXQRVNVCWSDrY` (the last epoch under the v2 rules, published at 2026-10-18 01:38 UTC) are paid under the v3 rules. No epoch under the v2 rules was published on 2026-11-22: that earlier publication is the last one. Under v3: each week's capacity is 8% of the free reward pool measured right after the previous publication, opened in seven daily tranches counted from that publication, and a day's slice is at most 2% of the XDEX pool's XNT reserve read at the previous build (at most 7 × 2% a week); burns are paid first come, first served inside it; the site and the kit show, before you sign, the part of your burn that fits today's remaining capacity, at your burn's own prices ("at most" until the epoch is built); each chain is valued on its own market with a liquidity curve that remembers recent burns on that chain (half-life 14 days), its liquidity fixed for the whole week at the previous epoch's build; Artifacts no longer change burn payouts (forged Artifacts get their own extra pot with the final program upgrade, docs/UPGRADE-2026-11.md). The first v3 epoch is published only by the upgraded program: if the upgrade comes later, so does that epoch; no epoch under the v2 rules follows the one above, and the burns made after it wait for the first v3 epoch with their own time. From 00:00 UTC on 2026-11-22 until this notice (2026-11-23 06:00 UTC), the site's Forge held new burns, because the last v2 publish transaction was not named yet; a burn made through another client in that time is paid under v3 if it is not in that last v2 epoch. The full rules: docs/spec/REWARDS-V3.md. Burns already paid, or listed in a published epoch, do not change. Payouts are not promised: they depend on the oracle publishing, on the capacity and on the XNT price. On 2026-11-02 the reward pool held about 6.5 XNT, so a whole week could pay all burners together at most about 0.5 XNT (about 0.07 XNT a day). The site shows the live numbers before you burn.

### Legacy Pilot (announced 2026-11-02)

**Rule** (`oracle/src/legacy.ts`). A one-time recognition for people who burned XEN before Moon Forge's final rules. Eligible: any EVM address that burned XEN (a transfer to the zero address recorded by XEN's own contract) on Ethereum, Base, Optimism, Polygon, BSC or Avalanche before 2026-11-01 00:00 UTC. From the first v3 epoch (the first epoch published on or after 2026-11-29; planned Sunday 2026-12-06), once the list is published and its hash pinned: (1) the address's first Moon Forge burn receives up to 50% more than its normal payout (a partial top-up also uses the address), and (2) 20% of the price paid for the first new Artifact (forged after the upgrade, in the week of such a burn or later) that an X1 wallet named in a Moon Forge burn of that address holds at a weekly snapshot, once per wallet and once per address, paid from the extra pot's weekly release that the holder rebates leave unused, oldest claim first (the extra pot comes with the final program upgrade, planned for 2026-12-02). Both are paid only from what normal payouts leave unused. One address, one benefit of each kind; operator wallets are excluded. Old burns are not paid here: a listed address receives a one-time top-up on its first new burn through a Moon Forge portal, from leftover capacity only.

**Disclosure.** Top-ups come only from capacity left over after every normal payout of the week, at most 25% of that leftover; nobody receives less in the week a top-up is paid; no top-up is paid in a week whose plan falls short (a shortfall week). That leftover would otherwise stay in the reward pool, and each week's capacity is 8% of the pool, so the pool grows slightly more slowly and some later weeks can have a slightly lower capacity. In the project's 104-week simulation, 4.75 XNT of top-ups meant 4.27 XNT less in total for later burners, and the Legacy Artifact rebates paid about 136 XNT from the extra pot's unused weekly release (figures of that simulation, not a forecast). Artifact rebates come only from the extra pot's weekly release that the holder rebates leave unused, within the program's limits (8% of the pot and 4% of the live backing of Artifacts forged after the upgrade per week); what a week cannot pay is paid in later weeks, oldest claim first. No payout is promised: in a week with no leftover no top-up is paid, and the address keeps its eligibility for a later burn.

**When it applies.** The list is `deployments/legacy-burners.json`, built by the public `Legacy Pilot snapshot` workflow; `node scripts/legacy/build-snapshot.mjs check deployments/legacy-burners.json` recomputes its sha256. The rule joins the first v3 rules block only if that sha256 is pinned in the oracle before the first v3 epoch is built; every epoch file built under it records the rule and the sha256 in its hashed rules block, and `npm run verify -- <file> --full` checks the listing. Otherwise it can only come later as a new rules version, announced here at least two epochs ahead (commitment 1), and it never applies backwards (commitment 2).

## Errata

If a published file and the published rules disagree, or the code that built a file is found wrong:

1. **Report.** Anyone reports it (a GitHub issue, or the project mailbox for anything sensitive, [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md#reporting-a-vulnerability)) with the epoch, the leaf or burn and the recomputation.
2. **Confirm.** The finding counts when it is reproduced from the public files with the published tools; the operator does not decide alone whether a recomputation is right.
3. **Record.** A correction note is added to this file (date, epoch, affected burns and leaves, the amounts, the cause) and to the next epoch announcement.
4. **No published epoch is ever changed.** An overpayment is not clawed back. An underpayment is topped up only from a source named in the correction note before any top-up is paid; a top-up never reduces another burner's arrival claim and never uses the capacity of a week already quoted. If no source is named, the correction note says so and nothing is paid.
5. **Code fixes.** A fix that only makes the code do what this file and the specification already say applies from the next epoch, announced with the correction note. A fix that changes a rule is a policy change under rule 1.

The only stops the oracle side has are not publishing an epoch and, from the final upgrade on, voiding a published epoch during its 48-hour hold under the program's bonded rules ([docs/UPGRADE-2026-11.md](docs/UPGRADE-2026-11.md#32-the-oracle-seat)).

## Operator policy

The operator is the role that runs the oracle and the site for this project. Wallets controlled by the operator are treated under these commitments from 2026-11-22 00:00 UTC (the start of the boundary hold announced on 2026-11-20):

- They never act on a rule or a number before it is published here or in an epoch file.
- Every X1 wallet the operator controls that receives a burn is listed in the epoch files' rules block (`operatorWallets`) before its first burn. Those burns are flagged in the file and rank after every other burn of the week, at arrival and in the leftover: they receive only what the week's capacity still holds after everyone else. The one exception is the mainnet proof plan: its wallets are a named exception in `operatorWallets` (the exception's name, the wallets, a start and an end time), published here with their chains, tiers and amounts before the first proof burn; inside that window their burns rank like any other burn and are flagged with the exception's name, outside it they rank last like every other operator wallet.
- The operator has no priority over other burners and no access to capacity, quotes or data that other burners do not have; the architect's income (none of it from burns or claims) is listed in [ARCHITECT.md](ARCHITECT.md).
- Epoch 1 (2026-10-18) contained a single burn. A single burn is not evidence of demand, and no epoch is presented as such.
